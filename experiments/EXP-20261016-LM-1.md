### Design set and replication set (decided)

Design = **RPE1** (`ReplogleWeissman2022_rpe1.h5ad`), replication = **K562 genome-wide**
(`ReplogleWeissman2022_K562_gwps.h5ad`), as D-022 states; #77 had the reverse and is superseded by

### Truth: per-TF knockdown response (fixed now, computed per screen)

- cells: obs label `control` = non-targeting controls (RPE1, #67; K562 check P2). Cells flagged as
  carrying more than one perturbation (if such a column exists, check P1) are dropped.
- control split (no control cell is used twice): control barcodes sorted, permuted with
  `np.random.default_rng(0)`; first half = inference pool, second half = truth controls. Truth uses
  3,000 truth controls drawn with `default_rng(1).choice(..., replace=False)`, sorted.
- normalisation for truth: counts / the cell's `core_scale_factor` (Replogle 2022's size factor from
  core genes; chosen by check P9's rule over counts / total x 10,000); `log1p` of that for the test.
  Cells without a positive factor are dropped (none in either screen).
- perturbation unit: pseudobulk per construct, obs `guide_id` (one id = one dual-sgRNA construct,
  check P1); most TFs have one construct, so the sign-agreement rule below rarely applies.
- knockdown efficiency of a guide g of TF t: KD_g = 1 - mean(t in g's cells) / mean(t in truth
  controls), on the normalised values. A guide is kept if KD_g >= 0.70 and it has >= 20 cells.
- TF cells: the cells of its kept guides, at most 200 (`default_rng(2).choice`, sorted) so that
  responder counts do not track cell numbers.
- responder test, per gene of the universe G (below) except t: two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum
  (`scipy.stats.mannwhitneyu`, asymptotic) of the TF cells against the truth controls on log1p values;
  Benjamini-Hochberg within the TF over all tested genes. A gene detected (count > 0) in < 5% of both
  groups is not tested and is a non-responder.
- responder: BH q < 0.05 **and** |log2FC| >= 0.25, log2FC = log2((m_t + 0.1) / (m_c + 0.1)) on the
  normalised pseudobulk means; with two or more kept guides, the per-guide pseudobulk log2FC must
  have the same sign in every kept guide. A cell-level test is used because in a pooled screen every
  perturbation is spread over the same lanes as the controls, so no sample-level confounder exists
  that a pseudobulk-replicate test would remove; the pseudobulk carries effect size and concordance.
- unit (TF) usable if: CollecTRI human source, in G, >= 1 kept guide, >= 11 responders in G. The
  truth step counts responders over every measured gene (check P3); the scoring step drops responders
  outside G and re-applies the >= 11 rule, so the analysed n is the scorer's count.
- the responders table (`tf, gene, q, log2fc, n_cells, kd`) is written and hashed before any network
  is scored.

### Inference (control cells only)

- input: the whole inference pool of control cells (not pre-capped); no perturbed cell is ever seen
  by a model.
- pipeline: the engine's fixed baseline (`screen.BASELINE_CHOICES`, D-021) with one declared change,
  `hvg = all_genes` instead of `tf_plus_1000`: the screen file already keeps only expressed genes, and
  a responder outside the network's genes could not be ranked. G = the genes of the processed matrix.
  The plan receives the CollecTRI sources present in the genes as `tf_list`, as `real_bench` does.
- cell cap: after the pipeline, at most 3,000 cells, `np.sort(np.random.default_rng(0).choice(n, 3000,
  replace=False))`, the `real_bench` rule (`MAX_SCORE_CELLS`).
- regulators: every CollecTRI human source in G (no `MAX_TFS` cap: every usable TF must be a
  regulator, and the set must not depend on the truth).
- models (exactly these): `pearson_signed`, `pearson_motif`, `grnboost2`, `grnboost2_motif`,
  `regdiffusion`, `regdiffusion_motif` (RegDiffusion stays: check P6 passed), and the motif controls
  `X_motif_genetf` = X's edges rank-producted with the TF-generic motif propensity (`gene_tf` of
  `benchmarks/motif_sensitivity.py`: for TF i, TF i's own sorted motif values reassigned by the
  leave-one-out mean motif rank over the other TFs with at least one motif (K > 0), as in GB-1; genes
  absent from the cisTarget database keep the worst rank; everything but TF-specific target identity).
  `_motif` = `cistarget.motif_rank_product` with the registered cisTarget v10 files.
- references: registered CollecTRI human snapshot and cisTarget v10 human (sha256 in
  `experiments/datasets.tsv`); screens by the pinned md5 of `fetch_perturb.py` plus a snapshot
  manifest at registration (D-020).

### Endpoint, controls and null

- primary endpoint: per unit TF t, AUPRC ratio (AP / prevalence) of t's ranking of every gene in G
  except t (missing edges share one bottom tie) against t's responders. AP is the expected AP when the
  order inside every tied block is random (exact closed form, McSherry and Najork 2008), the same
  convention as the top-k hit rates. Reason (independent review, 22/10): scored with one threshold per
  tied block, a sparse GRNBoost2 row (about 83% of genes in its bottom tie) lost 0.13 to 0.21 ratio units
  against its dense propensity from the tie convention alone, which would bias H2 for `grnboost2` and
  `grnboost2_motif`. Under this convention a random order does not score 1: its ratio depends on the
  number of responders (about 1.78 at 11 responders out of 8,000 genes, 1.03 at 300), so every report
  prints the random expectation computed for its own units. Code: `grnbench.eval.knockdown`
  (`metrics.expected_average_precision`).
- secondary endpoints: hit rate in the top 50 and top 200 (`precision_at_k`, k = 50, 200).
- gene-propensity control of every network X (`propensity:X`): for unit t, each gene's mean
  within-regulator percentile over every regulator of X except t (leave-one-TF-out in-strength). It
  uses no TF-specific information, so a network that only puts hub genes on top scores the same as its
  own propensity (tested on synthetic data: `tests/test_knockdown_eval.py`).
- response propensity (secondary): how often each gene responds to the other units' knockdowns
  (leave-one-TF-out), a model-free hub ranking.
- reference rows (descriptive): CollecTRI human scored as an edge list in the same way, and its own
  propensity; the random expectation (expected AP of a random order over each unit's responders, mean
  over units; prevalence for the hit rates).
- dependence-keeping null (D-022 (a)): joint TF-label permutation. One permutation pi of the unit TFs
  per draw, applied to every network arm at once (TF pi(i)'s ranking scored against TF i's
  responders); control arms stay on their unit. It keeps each network whole (hub genes, correlated TF
  rows, shared motifs) and each responder set whole (shared responders) and breaks only the pairing
  of a TF with its own knockdown. 10,000 permutations, seed 0.

### Hypotheses and comparison family (one family per screen)

All directional. For each model family X in {pearson, grnboost2, regdiffusion}:
- **H1a** `X_motif > X` (the motif prior lifts recovery of knockdown responders; C6 on causal truth).
- **H1b** `X_motif > X_motif_genetf` (the lift needs TF-specific motif identity, not a gene propensity).
- **H2** `M > propensity:M` for each of the six models M (M's per-TF rankings carry TF-specific
  information beyond its own hub structure).
- family size: 3 + 3 + 6 = 12 hypotheses, 24 directed, always: a hypothesis whose model failed stays in the
  family with p = 1 (it cannot be rejected, and Holm's multiplier does not shrink) and is reported as missing.
- test: for each hypothesis, the paired t over unit TFs from `compare_units` on the two sides of the
  contrast (one p per direction; 95% interval of the mean paired difference), and the joint TF-label
  permutation (one p per direction). Holm (`holm_adjust`) over all directed hypotheses of the family,
  separately for the t and for the permutation p-values, alpha 0.05. The permutation keeps each
  network's hub structure in the null (shown on synthetic data, `tests/test_knockdown_score.py`), so
  H1a supported means a lift beyond what mismatched TF pairing gives, not only a raw lift.
  For H2 the two p-values test different things. The paired t tests `M > propensity:M`. The permutation
  moves only M (control arms stay on their unit, so they shift the observed statistic and every draw by
  the same amount): it tests whether t's own row of M beats other TFs' rows of M on t's responders, i.e.
  TF-specific information in M. H2 supported needs both. H2 refuted would need t's own row to be worse
  than mismatched rows, which a network without TF-specific information does not show, so a model far
  below its propensity comes out inconclusive in practice; the reverse paired t is reported for it, and
  the record makes no claim from it.
- per screen: **supported** if both Holm p of the declared direction < 0.05; **refuted** if both Holm p
  of the reverse direction < 0.05; otherwise inconclusive (not evidence of equality).
- primary comparison: pearson_motif > pearson_signed | auprc_ratio | knockdown; pearson_motif > pearson_motif_genetf | auprc_ratio | knockdown; grnboost2_motif > grnboost2 | auprc_ratio | knockdown; grnboost2_motif > grnboost2_motif_genetf | auprc_ratio | knockdown; regdiffusion_motif > regdiffusion | auprc_ratio | knockdown; regdiffusion_motif > regdiffusion_motif_genetf | auprc_ratio | knockdown
- secondary (reported, outside the primary Holm): every model against the response propensity; top-50
  and top-200 hit rates, each with its own Holm family of the same hypotheses; the K562 essential and
  non-essential strata (check P5) and the TFs usable in both screens, each its own family on the
  primary endpoint (permutation within the stratum); TF detection: Spearman of each regulator's
  detected fraction in the capped inference cells against its per-unit score, per arm, descriptive;
  the sensitivity analysis declared in check P9.
- smallest effect that matters: none declared. What the design can detect is check P11: at 80% power, a
  standardised paired effect (dz = mean paired difference / SD of the differences) of about 0.46 in both
  screens jointly at the strictest Holm step, about 0.31 at the last step; a smaller true effect may come out
  inconclusive, which is never read as "no difference".

### Decision rule (outcome -> verdict -> what the paper may say)

A hypothesis is a result only if it has the same verdict in RPE1 and K562 (`knockdown.combine_sets`).
- H1a and H1b supported in both: "the motif prior raises recovery of knockdown responders, and the
  gain needs TF-specific motif identity" (new claim, `confirmed`, then reproduction).
- H1a supported in both, H1b not: "the motif lift on knockdown truth is consistent with a TF-generic
  gene propensity" (extends the GB-1 caveat to causal truth; no TF-specific motif claim).
- H1a refuted in both: the prior lowers recovery of causal responders; C6 is limited to agreement with
  the literature in the text.
- H2 supported in both for model M: "M's targets predict knockdown responses beyond gene propensity".
  H2 inconclusive for every model ("every model" = every model that ran): "no evidence, in either
  screen, that networks inferred from control cells predict knockdown responses beyond gene
  propensity", reported with the intervals and the P11 power, never as "they do not predict" (an
  inconclusive test is not evidence of equality). H2 refuted in both for model M (unlikely by design,
  see the test above): "M ranks knockdown responders below its own gene propensity", reported, not a
  claim. H1a and H1b are judged per model family X, H2 per model. A hypothesis that is supported or
  refuted in one screen and inconclusive or missing in the other is "not replicated" (next line), in
  either order of the screens.
- any verdict in one screen only: "not replicated", reported with both sets' numbers, no claim.
- inconclusive anywhere: reported as inconclusive, never as "no difference".

### Exclusions (decided now)

(a) TFs that are not CollecTRI sources, not in G, without a kept guide, or with < 11 responders are not
units (listed with the reason, `evaluate()["excluded"]`). (b) Cells with more than one perturbation
are dropped (if flagged). (c) A model that errors or does not finish within the job and one
resubmission of the same commit: the first inference with a failed model is `incomplete` and fails the job
(nothing is published); the resubmission skips the finished steps and reruns it, and only a second failure of the
same arm on the same commit is published. A failure is a caught error or a job that dies inside the arm (Slurm
timeout or kill): before an arm starts the script writes `started` for it, with the commit and the time, to
`infer_attempts.json` (atomic replace; `done` or `failed` when it ends), and the next run of the same commit counts
every arm still `started` as one failed attempt (`infer.json["interrupted"]`). Two such runs, or one kill and one
caught error, are the second failure: the arm is then not run again and is published as missing; a different
commit starts from zero. A kill between two arms of one base model (base, `_motif`, `_genetf`) counts for all of
that base's arms. Its hypotheses are then reported as missing (listed), and Holm still
runs over all 24 directed hypotheses with p = 1 for them. A resubmission must match `run.json` (commit, snapshot
hash, models, permutations, normalisation) or the job stops. (d) A screen whose md5 or snapshot check fails is
not analysed. (e) No exclusion based on any score, no outlier removal.

## 2. How it runs

- code: git commit at registration (clean tree; on the HPC the `GIT_COMMIT` file of `hpc/hpc.sh sync`).
  Evaluator: `src/grnbench/eval/knockdown.py`; runner: `benchmarks/knockdown_bench.py`,
  `benchmarks/knockdown_score.py`; job: `hpc/knockdown_run.sbatch`.
- environment: `requirements/lock-py311.txt`, Python 3.11, torch and regdiffusion as in the lock header
  (P6 kept RegDiffusion).
- data: the D-020 snapshot root `~/grnbench_data_kd1` (check P12); the job stops unless
  `data_manifest.py <root> --check experiments/EXP-20261016-LM-1.snapshot.tsv --require-all` passes.
- command(s), from a laptop at the repo root, on the registration commit with a clean tree:
  ```
  python -m grnbench.board                       # no open `running` issue for this EXP
  bash hpc/hpc.sh check && bash hpc/hpc.sh sync  # GIT_COMMIT = the registration commit
  bash hpc/hpc.sh submit hpc/knockdown_run.sbatch
  gh issue edit <claim issue> --add-label running && gh issue comment <claim issue> --body "launched: HPC job <id>"
  # after the job: bash hpc/hpc.sh status <id>; resubmit the same line once if it failed (exclusion (c))
  bash hpc/hpc.sh fetch "~/lm_runs/EXP-20261016-LM-1" <local folder outside the repo>
  ```
  Inside the job, per screen: `knockdown_bench.py subset`, `truth --norm core`, `total-shift`, `units`; then
  `controls --norm core` (the whole inference pool, the complement of the truth half under the same
  control mask; `score` refuses to run if any control cell is in both, from `cells.txt` and
  `truth_half.txt`, or if inference and truth come from different screens), `knockdown_score.py infer --experiment EXP-20261016-LM-1 --device cpu`, `score --perms 10000
  --seed 0 --collectri <CollecTRI> --both-with <other screen's truth> --total-shift <total_shift.tsv>` (K562 adds
  `--essential-from <RPE1 truth>`, check P5); finally `combine --design <RPE1 score> --replication <K562 score>`.
- machine: Bocconi HPC, QOS stud, one job for both screens, 8 CPUs, 64 GB, 24 h wall, working files on the
  node-local disk, finished step folders under `~/lm_runs/EXP-20261016-LM-1`; RegDiffusion on CPU (see seeds).
- compute estimate per job (scaled from EXP-20261002-LM-1, where per dataset GRNBoost2 took 57 s and
  RegDiffusion 378 s on about 1,150 genes, 150 regulators and <= 3,000 cells; P6 measures it):
  RPE1: truth about 30 min (154 perturbed TFs, 200 vs 3,000 cells over about 8,700 genes); baseline
  pipeline and Pearson minutes; GRNBoost2 about 0.5-2 h (genes x7.6, regulators x(R/150)); RegDiffusion
  about 50 min per fit on 8 CPU cores (P6); motif and `_genetf` arms minutes;
  scoring about 7 min per screen for three endpoint families (measured at RPE1 size on synthetic data)
  plus about 4 min for the strata; K562 has fewer regulators and genes, more units (104 to 109).
- results: `benchmarks/results/EXP-20261016-LM-1/{rpe1,k562}/` (responders table, per-unit scores,
  family JSON, report); edges and processed matrices stay on the cluster under `~/lm_runs/EXP-20261016-LM-1`.
- report input: `benchmarks/results/EXP-20261016-LM-1`
